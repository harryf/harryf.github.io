The reality check, in five lines

  1. Zürich city vacancy is 0.11 % (252 empty flats on 1 June 2026). Good flats are re-let within hours, so speed and a ready dossier beat everything else.
  2. Open-market asking rent for a 2.5-room flat in the city averages about CHF 2800. The cheapest district (Kreis 12, Schwamendingen) averages about CHF 1850. At CHF 1500, a city 2.5-room flat is a rare outlier: old building, ground floor, busy road, or a Nachmieter (successor tenant) hand-over.
  3. CHF 1300 to 1500 is normal only in three places: Genossenschaften (housing cooperatives) and city-owned flats, the outer agglomeration (Limmattal, Uster, Bülach, Baden, Winterthur), or 1.5 to 2-room flats of 40 m² or more.
  4. Night transport is weekend-only. The ZVV Nachtnetz (night buses and night S-Bahn) runs only on Friday and Saturday nights and before public holidays, with a normal ticket (the CHF 5 night surcharge was abolished in 2021). On Sunday to Thursday nights the last S-Bahn leaves HB at about 00:39, trams stop around 00:45, and service restarts between about 04:30 and 05:20. After a Tuesday close at 01:00 it is walk, bike, or taxi. Distance from ROBINs has a real cash value on weeknights.
  5. Run two tracks at once. Short term: alerts plus a same-day dossier in the realistic areas. Long term: register with the cooperatives now, because their allocations are effectively lotteries that you can only win by being in them for years.

Getting home at night from ROBINs

Friday and Saturday nights

The ZVV Nachtnetz runs from about 01:00 until the day network restarts around 04:30, mostly hourly (SN1 and SN6 to Winterthur and the city night buses every 30 minutes). Night S-Bahn from HB: SN1 and SN6 (Altstetten, Schlieren, Dietikon, Baden, Winterthur), SN5 (Uster, Wetzikon, Affoltern am Albis), SN7 (Oerlikon, Kloten, Meilen), SN8 (Wallisellen, Wollishofen, Thalwil, Wädenswil), SN9 (Oerlikon, Bülach, Dübendorf, Uster). Night buses from HB and Central: N1 and N3 (Altstetten, Albisrieden), N5 (Wiedikon, Sihlcity), N7 (Schwamendingen, Stettbach), N10 (Oerlikon, Kloten), N13 and N46 (Wipkingen, Höngg), N16 (Wollishofen, Thalwil), N17 (Schlieren), N71 (Witikon direction, Forch). Zug has no night S-Bahn. From ROBINs the whole Nachtnetz is a two to five-minute walk away, which is a genuine location advantage.

Ticket: a normal ZVV ticket or pass for your zones. The CHF 5 Nachtzuschlag was abolished when the Nachtnetz restarted in July 2021. Departure times checked against the September 2026 timetable; recheck after the December timetable change.

Sunday to Thursday nights

No Nachtnetz. From Central the last trams leave between about 00:30 and 00:45. From HB the last S-Bahn departures are around 00:14 (S12 to Dietikon and Baden), 00:19 (S7 Kloten and Winterthur), 00:21 (S24 Wollishofen), 00:24 (Dübendorf, Uster), 00:25 (S8 Oerlikon, Wallisellen, Seebach) and 00:39 (S5 Altstetten, Schlieren, Zug). First trains run from about 04:33 to 05:20. So finishing by 00:15 keeps almost everything open; finishing after about 00:40 on a weeknight means no train or tram until 04:30.

Rough weeknight fares from Niederdorf (city maximum taxi tariff is CHF 8 plus CHF 5 per km): taxi to Oerlikon or Altstetten about CHF 30 to 35, Schlieren or Dübendorf CHF 50 to 60, Dietikon or Uster CHF 65 to 85, Baden or Winterthur CHF 100 and up. Uber without surge is roughly half: Oerlikon or Altstetten CHF 16 to 20, Schlieren or Dübendorf CHF 25 to 32. Orders of magnitude, not quotes.

What this means for the search

  • Within about 3 km (Kreis 1, 4, 5, 6, parts of 3, 10 and 11) you are always independent: 15 to 25 minutes on foot or 10 minutes by bike.
  • Within about 6 km (Altstetten, Oerlikon, Seebach, Schwamendingen, Wollishofen, Höngg, Wallisellen, Schlieren edge) a bike or e-bike makes any night workable: 20 to 35 minutes, flat routes along the Limmat and Glatt valleys.
  • Beyond that (Dietikon, Dübendorf, Uster, Baden, Winterthur, Bülach) the rent is right but weeknight closes need a plan: a taxi budget, staff accommodation in town, or a shift pattern where late closes fall on Fridays and Saturdays.
  • PubliBike (Züri Velo, about 200 stations, 24/7 via app) covers the city districts but not Schlieren, Dübendorf or Uster, and e-bike availability after 01:00 is patchy; the CHF 69 per year local pass is cheap insurance for Oerlikon, Altstetten, Wollishofen or Höngg. Your own e-bike (CHF 1500 to 3000 once, or leasing) pays for itself against a taxi habit within a few months.

Short to medium term: land a flat in the next few weeks

1. Build the dossier once, as one PDF, before you search

  • Betreibungsregisterauszug (debt-collection register extract), not older than 3 months, ordered online from the Betreibungsamt of your current commune
  • Lohnausweis or your last 3 payslips, plus the employment contract from ROBINs
  • ID or passport, and Ausländerausweis (residence permit) if applicable
  • Short reference letter from your current landlord, or at least their contact
  • Last tax bill if you have one
  • Half-page cover letter: who you are, that you have worked at ROBINs for N years, non-smoker or not, no pets, quiet single tenant, long-term intent
  • Optional: a photo and a line from your employer confirming stable employment

Landlords apply a rule of thumb: gross rent no more than about a third of net income. CHF 1500 implies about CHF 4500 net per month. If your net is lower, prioritise Genossenschaft and CHF 1300 targets and be ready to show savings or a guarantor.

2. Alerts everywhere, apply within the hour

  • Free email alerts on flatfox, homegate, ImmoScout24 and comparis with: ≤ CHF 1500, ≥ 2.5 rooms; and a second alert ≤ CHF 1500, ≥ 40 m², any rooms.
  • Subscribe to the Stadt Zürich Vermietungen newsletter (city-owned flats, viewing slots are drawn by lottery).
  • Nachmieter (successor tenant) ads on Ron Orp and tutti: the way into old, cheap existing rents. Since 1 October 2025 landlords in Kanton Zürich must hand new tenants a form stating the previous rent; a big jump can be challenged within 30 days.
  • Tell every regular at the bar you are looking. In Zürich a surprising share of cheap flats change hands by word of mouth before they are listed.
  • Untermiete (subletting) only with the landlord's written consent, and know that you have almost no protection if the main lease ends. Fine as a bridge, not as the plan.

3. Where to point the alerts

Best odds for CHF 1500 and a workable night commute, in order: Schwamendingen (Kreis 12), Affoltern and Seebach (Kreis 11), Altstetten and Albisrieden (Kreis 9), Höngg, Schlieren, then Dietikon and Urdorf. Slightly further but cheap and well connected: Uster, Bülach, Regensdorf, Kloten, Baden, Winterthur.

Accept a 2-room or a large 1.5-room flat of 40 m² or more if it lands near CHF 1300 in a green or amber area. Rooms are a proxy; the square metres and the commute are what you will live with.

If a place needs a decision on the spot, a competitive application in 2026 means: complete PDF dossier attached to the first message, available for a viewing at any time including the next morning, and a one-line reason the landlord should pick you (stable job, single, quiet, long-term).

Long term: a Genossenschaft (housing cooperative) flat for the next 5 to 10 years

About a quarter of Zürich's flats are cooperative or city-owned, rented at cost, typically 20 to 40 % below the private market. This is where CHF 1300 buys a real flat. The catch is access, so the strategy is to be visible to as many cooperatives as possible, for as long as possible, while the short-term track keeps you housed.

How cooperatives actually allocate flats

  • Anteilschein (share certificate): when you get a flat you buy shares in the cooperative, refunded when you leave. Typical: ABZ CHF 3500 for 1.5 rooms and CHF 4500 for 2.5 rooms; Wogeno CHF 3000; ASIG CHF 3000 to 9000 by size; Kalkbreite about CHF 260 per m² on moving in. Several cooperatives let you join as a member before you have a flat (Kraftwerk1 CHF 550, Kalkbreite CHF 1200, mehr als wohnen CHF 1000, Wogeno CHF 3000), and only members see their vacancies.
  • Belegungsvorschriften (occupancy rules): the standard Zürich rule is "rooms minus one equals minimum persons", so as a single person you qualify for 1 to 2.5 rooms and are rejected automatically for 3 rooms or more. A few cooperatives are stricter for singles. Aim your coop search at 1.5 to 2.5 rooms and lead with 2 to 2.5.
  • No waiting lists (mostly): ABZ, FGZ, PWG, Sonnengarten, Sunnige Hof, mehr als wohnen and the city all say "keine Warteliste". Vacancies go on their own sites (FGZ at 16:00, Sonnengarten on Tuesday mornings, PWG for at least 72 hours) and on flatfox, homegate or newhome, often for only 15 to 30 minutes. Viewings are frequently drawn at random (ABZ draws 15 applicants; the city draws viewing slots). Member-only cooperatives (Kraftwerk1, Kalkbreite, mehr als wohnen, Wogeno) are the exception: Wogeno itself says 6 to 9 years before you are even invited to an allocation meeting. Being a member early is the real waiting list.
  • Income and asset limits: city-owned flats at cost rent require household income no more than four times the gross annual rent at entry (about CHF 72 000 for a CHF 1500 flat) and cap it at CHF 70 000 later; subsidised flats cap taxable income at about CHF 53 000 for a single person and require two years' residence in the canton. Standard cooperative flats mostly have occupancy rules only, though several say lower income is an advantage.
  • Participation: many cooperatives expect a little engagement (assemblies, working groups). Saying you are up for it in the application helps.
  • WG inside a cooperative (a shared flat in a Kraftwerk1, Kalkbreite or Dreieck building) is a legitimate foot in the door: you become a member and resident, and internal moves to a single flat are often easier than external applications.

Start this week

  1. Bookmark the wbg-zh.ch flat-search platform (the umbrella body of Zürich cooperatives) and read its application tips.
  2. Create an account on the city's E-Vermietung portal, read the Mietbedingungen, and apply to every 1.5 to 2.5-room city flat that appears (new ones roughly weekly); viewing slots are drawn at random, so volume matters.
  3. Pay the memberships that start your seniority: Kraftwerk1 (CHF 550), Kalkbreite (CHF 1200), mehr als wohnen (CHF 1000), Wogeno (CHF 3000, three-month waiting period before you can apply). About CHF 5750 in total, all refundable when you leave or never move in. If cash is tight, Kraftwerk1 first.
  4. Register on the free open waiting lists (ASIG, and smaller ones such as BG Alpenblick, BG Kyburg, Baufreunde, GB Riesbach, BG Glattal, Hofgarten, Rotach), and take voluntary membership at Sunnige Hof for priority tier two.
  5. Set flatfox, homegate and newhome alerts with the same filters; most cooperatives list there. Add one paid aggregator that watches 90 to 160 cooperative sites for you: Genossenschaftsscout (CHF 5 per month, email and Telegram), GenossenSchaffen (CHF 5), gnossiZH (CHF 4.50) or Genossenschafts Radar (CHF 9.90).
  6. Watch the new-build pipeline: Koch-Areal in Altstetten (ABZ, Kraftwerk1) let its first round in June 2026 and moves people in from October 2026 to mid 2027, so dropped-out units will be re-let via the ABZ newsletter and abz-koch.ch; Hardturm "Ensemble" (ABZ, 174 flats) completes around 2030; Thurgauerstrasse (Wogeno, about 160 flats) around 2034, which is exactly why joining Wogeno now makes sense.
  7. Keep a spreadsheet: cooperative, membership status, date joined, applications sent. Update it every month. Realistic waits are 3 to 10 years in the city and markedly shorter in Limmattal and Glattal cooperatives, so the agglomeration branches (BEP in Urdorf, Wallisellen, Dietikon; Sonnengarten in Dietikon, Weiningen, Geroldswil; ASIG in Rümlang) are the fast lane.

Questions for Elias

Your answers change the map and the filters. Reply to Harry with the numbers and a line each; the page gets regenerated from your answers.

  1. When does your current lease end, and how flexible is your move-in date? Decides how aggressive the short-term track has to be and whether a bridge (Untermiete, Zwischenmiete) is worth it.
  2. Which nights do you usually close late, and how late? Is it mostly Friday and Saturday, or spread across the week? Can you ever be at HB by 00:39 on a weeknight? If late closes are mostly weekends, the Nachtnetz covers you and the amber and red areas open up. On weeknights the last S-Bahn leaves HB at 00:39; after that distance costs real money.
  3. Would you commute by bike or e-bike, including in winter and at 02:00? A yes turns everything within 6 km into "any night" territory and roughly doubles the number of realistic areas.
  4. What is your net monthly income and taxable income, roughly, and do you have savings for an Anteilschein (CHF 3000 to 9000) plus a deposit (up to three months' rent)? Could you put about CHF 5750 into refundable cooperative memberships now? Landlords screen at about one third of net income; city flats have an income ceiling (roughly CHF 70 000); cooperatives need share capital on signing, and early memberships are what start the clock.
  5. Is CHF 1500 including Nebenkosten (heating, water, building costs), or the net rent? Nebenkosten add CHF 150 to 250. It changes which listings qualify.
  6. Would you take a large 1.5 or 2-room flat (40 m² or more) if it were in a better location or cheaper than a 2.5? This is the most common way single people get into cooperatives.
  7. Would you consider a WG room inside a cooperative (Kraftwerk1, Kalkbreite, Dreieck, mehr als wohnen) as a route to a single flat later? It is often the fastest way to become a member and resident.
  8. Any hard no's: ground floor, no lift, busy road, no balcony, no bath, smoker building, pets? Cheap city flats almost always carry one of these compromises; knowing which are acceptable filters fast.
  9. Do you have a car, or need parking? Removes or adds CHF 100 to 250 per month and changes the outer-area picture.
  10. Are you registered in Zürich already (Wohnsitz), for how many years, and what is your permit status (Swiss, B, C)? Subsidised city flats require two years' residence in the canton and a Swiss, B, C or S permit; it also affects the dossier.
  11. How many years have you been at ROBINs, and could the owner write a one-paragraph employer reference? Stable employment plus a personal reference is the strongest thing a bar worker can put in a Zürich dossier.
  12. Which areas do you already know and like or dislike? The map has more candidates than anyone can chase; your gut narrows it.
  13. Furnished or unfurnished? Do you own furniture? Unfurnished is cheaper and much more common; furnished flats skew temporary.
  14. Are you already a member of, or on a list with, any cooperative? Avoid duplicate registrations and count the years you already have banked.

How this page updates

This is one static HTML file hosted on GitHub Pages. Two scripts fill the "Possible places" section: Tools/CrawlPortals.ts drives Harry's own Chrome (via the Interceptor extension) through the ImmoScout24 result pages, and Tools/UpdateListings.ts queries the flatfox public API, merges both feeds, deduplicates them and rewrites the data block inside this file. Tools/UpdateAll.sh runs both every morning from cron on Harry's machine and publishes the result. Between runs the script keeps listings.state.json: a listing seen for the first time is marked new for 48 hours, a flatfox listing that has disappeared is removed at once, and a portal listing that the partial crawl did not re-see is kept for three days, then removed. Static content (areas, cooperatives, questions) is regenerated from UPDATE_PROMPT.md after Elias answers the questions. Alerts (push or messaging when a new match appears) are the planned next step.

Listings shown are whatever flatfox had at the last refresh; a listing can be gone within hours. If the flatfox API changes, the script fails loudly and the page keeps the last good snapshot with its timestamp.
