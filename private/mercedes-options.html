    Read this before driving to Däniken

    1. The ad contradicts itself. The "Unfallfahrzeug" field says no, the description says right-side damage. The last gallery image shows scrapes along the right front door and a dent with paint damage above the right rear wheel arch. The rear quarter panel is a welded panel, not a bolt-on. That is the one thing that decides whether this is a bargain or a trap.
    2. The price is the damage. The cheapest undamaged C 220 d 4Matic estate on AutoScout24 today has 247,000 km and asks CHF 7,999. At 146,800 km, CHF 8,999 only makes sense because of the side damage.
    3. 170 PS means the older OM651 2.1-litre engine, not the 194 PS OM654 that the facelift got in 2018. Not a problem, but it changes what to check (timing chain, injector seals, AdBlue system). Confirm the engine on the Fahrzeugausweis.

    3. What to check on the car

    Order matters. Any declared damage first, then the engine, then the drivetrain, then the paperwork. All ten cars on this page use the same Mercedes OM651 diesel family (1.8 or 2.1 litre), so the engine card applies to every one of them.

    Decides the deal on the Däniken car

    Right-side damage

    • Rear quarter panel above the wheel arch: is it a dent in the original panel, or has it been filled and painted? Paint depth gauge on the quarter, the door, and the sill, compared with the left side. Anything over about 200 microns or wildly uneven means filler.
    • Inner wheel arch and sill from underneath: creases, fresh underseal, or a bent jacking point mean the hit went deeper than the skin.
    • Rear door and tailgate gaps: even gap top to bottom, door closes without lifting, tailgate seals evenly.
    • Right front door: scrapes only, or a dent that pulls the door skin? Open it fully and feel along the crease line.
    • Wheels and alignment: check the right rear alloy for kerb damage. On the test drive the car must track straight with hands off at 80 km/h. A rear-wheel hit often bends a control arm.
    • Glass and trim: right-side window seals, mirror, and the B-pillar cover; replacements without the Mercedes marking suggest a bigger repair.
    • Airbag light: must come on with ignition and go off. A side hit can trigger the seat or curtain airbag. If any airbag deployed and the ad says "Unfallfahrzeug: Nein", walk away.
    Get a body shop quote for the quarter panel before agreeing a price. A proper repair and paint on the rear quarter plus door is realistically CHF 2,000 to 4,000, which is your negotiating number. On the undamaged alternatives, use this list anyway on every panel: a cheap Mercedes with a repaint somewhere is normal, a repaint nobody mentioned is not.
    Engine, OM651 diesel (all ten cars)

    Known OM651 weak points

    • Timing chain: cold start rattle for the first two seconds is the sign of a stretched chain. Ask to start it stone cold, so do not let them warm it up before you arrive.
    • Injector seals ("black death"): pull the engine cover and look for black tar-like carbon around the injector bases. Also smell for exhaust under the bonnet at idle.
    • AdBlue system (2015 and newer cars): check for any AdBlue warning or a start countdown in the cluster. NOx sensors, the tank heater, and the metering unit are the usual expensive failures. Ask when the tank was last filled.
    • DPF and EGR: most of these cars have 150,000 to 250,000 km of unknown use. Check for a DPF load reading or at least a limp-free hard acceleration under load without smoke. A regeneration that never completes means short trips.
    • Oil leaks: oil cooler seals and the vacuum pump area at the front of the head. Look for wet lower block and sump.
    • Turbo: whistle or blue smoke on hard acceleration, and play in the wastegate arm.
    • Glow plugs and cold start smoke: white smoke on a cold start that clears is fine, persistent is not.
    Drivetrain

    Gearboxes and 4Matic

    • 9G-Tronic and 7G-Tronic (torque converter, C, E, GLK): shifts should be invisible on a gentle drive. Flare between 2 and 3, a thump on downshift to 1 when stopping, or hunting at 50 km/h are the warning signs. Ask for proof of the transmission oil and filter service; Mercedes calls for it around 125,000 km, and at the mileages here it is either done or due.
    • 7G-DCT (dual clutch, B, CLA, GLA): creep from standstill must be smooth, no judder or shunt in stop-start traffic, no hesitation when going from reverse to drive. Fluid change is due every 60,000 km or so; a DCT with no service history is the expensive one. A clutch actuator or mechatronic unit is a four-figure repair.
    • 4Matic: listen for a whine that changes with speed and not engine revs. On full lock at walking pace there must be no binding or clunking. Front driveshaft CV boots intact, no clicking on full lock.
    • Tyres: all four the same size and ideally the same brand and wear. Mismatched tyres on a 4Matic wear the transfer case. Note the DOT dates.
    • Brakes: discs for lips and scoring, and check the electric handbrake (where fitted) releases cleanly.
    • Suspension: bounce each corner, listen for knocks over speed bumps.
    Electronics and body

    Assistance systems the ad promises

    • Camera, blind spot, lane keeping, Attention Assist, PDC: switch each one on and confirm it works. A side hit can damage a radar or camera and the ad still lists them as working.
    • Diagnostic scan: if you can bring Xentry or a decent scanner, read all modules. Stored crash events in the airbag module (SRS) and a mismatched VIN in any module are deal breakers.
    • Electric tailgate, seat heating, navigation, both keys: test each. Two working keys should start the car; one key means budget CHF 300 to 400.
    • Water ingress: lift the boot floor and the spare wheel well, and feel the rear footwells.
    • Rust: tailgate lower edge, sills behind the plastic covers, and on the older W212 and GLK the front wings and boot lid seams.
    Paperwork

    Documents that must match

    • Fahrzeugausweis: VIN matches the plate on the B-pillar and the windscreen. Note the engine code, Hubraum, Gesamtgewicht, and the number of previous Halter.
    • Service book or digital service record (DSB): Mercedes went digital around 2008. Ask the seller to print it or check it yourself at a Mercedes dealer by VIN. Gaps after about 100,000 km are common on cheap cars.
    • Last MFK report: read the Mängel list, not just the stamp. "Ab MFK" in an ad only means the seller says it passed recently.
    • "CH-Fahrzeug" or import: a Swiss-market car (Swiss Star editions always are) is easier to insure and to sell on. Confirm on the Ausweis.
    • Kaufvertrag: mileage, damage described, "Ab MFK", keys, and the price. Sign nothing that says "gekauft wie gesehen" without any damage itemised.
    Negotiation

    How to price it

    Every C 220 d 4Matic estate on the market under CHF 12,000 today is either damaged or past 240,000 km, so the Däniken car's price is the damage, nothing else. The nine alternatives are all undamaged (per the ads) but older or smaller. If the Däniken quarter panel is skin-deep and the gearbox has had its oil service, CHF 8,999 is defensible. If not, the B 220 CDI Swiss Star in Nürensdorf with a July 2026 MFK is the cleanest same-day buy on this page.
