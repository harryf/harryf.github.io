The honest read

  1. The Brew is the business, and it has started to grow. Three years flat at about 20 a night, then 30 a night since August: the ads, the daily titles and the thank-you emails did that. Half the room comes back, which is what a club needs. It is also the only thing paying for everything else (about CHF 8,000 gross a year, CHF 5,000 after ads).
  2. You already have the throughput you once thought paid a salary. The voice note says 350 to 400 people a month. Comedy already brings roughly 450 a month into ROBIN's. The spreadsheet and the Elias model say the real bar is about 350 a week (1,500 a month) before a rented room pays one wage. You are at a third of that, and the gap is the whole strategy.
  3. Guest series die without an owner who sells. Offside Comedy (July, weekly, nobody's audience) went 12, 3, 14, 6 and stopped. Tall Order shared Tuesdays with Jokes and was folded in after two nights. Brexiles at Monroe fell from 53 to 14 over fourteen months. The ones that work have a named person whose own followers show up: Promessi (Miguel and Nik), Double Shot (you and Martina), Jokes (Woocash). Cadence is the other killer: a weekly show needs four times the audience of a monthly one.
  4. Promessi Spassi is a different kind of show, not a bigger version of the same thing. One night a month, a language community, two hosts with their own Instagram, and 3,280 ticket clicks on the site since August without a franc of IN YOUR FACE ad money. It is the second strongest demand signal on the site after the Brew. The repeatable part is the template (community, monthly, owner with reach), not the Italian.
  5. Fridays and Saturdays are empty, and that is where a club's money is. The spreadsheet's optimistic year gets 60% of its ticket income from Friday and Saturday house shows at 60 to 70 a night. Today there are none. One Double Shot proved the price point, not the night.
  6. The full club does not add up yet, and will not in 2027. CHF 21,850 a month of fixed cost plus CHF 180,000 up front. The spreadsheet's base case loses CHF 71,500 a year on 13,190 people; the optimistic case makes CHF 94,000 on 23,620 people, which is four times today. Nobody should sign a CHF 20,000 lease on a hope of 4x. The small room (CHF 9,000 rent) is a café business that only works with Elias, and it needs the same throughput to make comedy matter.
  7. The limiting resource is your hours, not the room. You host every Thursday and run the whole back office alone. Every path below is sized in hours a week, and the plan's first hire is not staff, it is two co-organisers.

Four paths, plus the one you asked to keep open

A. Venue partner, as now 5 to 8 h a week

Keep the Brew, let producers run shows for free, ROBIN's keeps the drinks. No rent, no risk, no leverage. Setup pain every night stays.

Right when: growth stalls below 700 a month for two check-ins, or your hours have to drop. This is the "not a club" answer and it is a fine one: a profitable hobby with a room that likes you.

B. Resident programme recommended now 8 to 12 h, with two helpers

Same deal with ROBIN's (no rent, they keep the bar) but run it like a club: a permanent stage corner with the PA left in place, one producer agreement, a monthly Friday house show, one more community show, every show counted, one shared mailing list. Prove the club before paying for it.

Right when: now. It costs a few thousand francs of kit and a conversation with ROBIN's, and it produces the numbers paths C and D need.

C. Take the small room gate: 1,000 a month and Elias 20+ h

CHF 9,000 rent for the front room, run the bar, the owners' preferred version. The voice note says it clears CHF 4,000 to 5,000 a month as a café; that needs six months of till data to believe. Comedy adds to it only above about 350 guests a week.

Right when: 1,000 a month for three months, Elias signed, till data seen, and you want to run a bar.

D. The comedy club gate: 1,500 a month for 6 months a job

Back room at CHF 20,000 a month, CHF 180,000 up front, drinks are yours, parties until late. Break-even sits near 1,500 people a month with the weekend mix the spreadsheet assumes.

Right when: every gate in the list below is green for two quarters. Earliest honest date: 2028.

The recommendation. Run path B for twelve months with the check-ins below. At the January 2028 check-in, the numbers pick between A, C and D for you. The plan is written so that nothing done under B is wasted if the answer is A.

The series rules (put them in the producer agreement)

  • One owner. A named person whose followers come. No owner, no slot.
  • Three-show trial, then the number talks. Paid show: trailing average of four below 15, it pauses. Free show: below 25. A show that drops two months running gets a conversation, not a surprise.
  • Cadence follows the number. Monthly until 40 a night, fortnightly until 40 again, weekly only above that. The Brew took three years to earn weekly numbers.
  • One show per weeknight. Two producers on the same night fight for the same 30 people.
  • Count every show. Headcount the same night, and the till total from Elias.
  • The list is shared. A door QR to one "Comedy at ROBIN's" list, run by you; a producer cannot hand over their buyers' emails (data protection), but they can put the QR on the door and in their thank-you message.

The Promessi factor

What it tells you, and what to do with it:

  • It is community demand, not comedy demand. Italians in Zürich have few nights that are theirs. The hosts bring their followers; the format is an open mic. The same mechanism explains the Spanish and Balkan nights that have come and gone, and the Beast in Basel is built on it with the English-speaking crowd.
  • The sustainability risk is real and dated. Novelty carries a community show for 12 to 18 months, then it needs new faces or a new hook (Brexiles fell from 53 to 14 by month fourteen). Keep it monthly so each night stays an event, and read its count every month in the tracker. A fall below 60 for two months is the signal to help, not to replace.
  • Replicate the template, not the show. One monthly night per language community, each owned by a host with reach: Spanish (Andrea already runs La Tarima elsewhere in town), German (the Digital Daddy night in April is the test), French (March). Four communities at 60 to 100 each is 300 a month, producer-owned, with few of your hours. That is the single biggest realistic lever in this plan.
  • It is worth more than its drinks to you. 100 people in the room is the proof ROBIN's and any future landlord need. Ask Miguel and Nik for the headcount and for the door QR, and give them something back: the Friday house show slot once a quarter, or the first Saturday under the touring offer.

Fridays and Saturdays: selective, not open

  • House Friday, monthly. Double Shot showed 23 at CHF 19 with no track record. Make it a brand ("Friday Night Live" in the spreadsheet), rotate the second host, lineup from the Brew's strongest. Target 40 by June 2027, 50 by December.
  • Partner Saturday, when it comes. The Beast is a members club in Basel running English nights most weeks; Red Mic tours "best of" line-ups through Basel, Geneva and a Zürich date at a club in Kreis 4 in October. The offer is simple: the room, organic promotion, a listing; they ticket and pay for ads. Two or three of these a quarter is plenty; it makes the room's name without your hours.
  • Not open to anyone. The ComedyHaus (200 seats, hire on request) is the place for a comedian with a budget. Your filter is the owner rule plus one referee you trust. A bad Saturday costs more reputation than an empty one.
  • Late show later. The spreadsheet's 22:30 "drinkers' show" belongs to path D, when the bar is yours. Under B it only makes ROBIN's money and costs your Friday.
